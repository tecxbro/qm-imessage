## Candidate audit and repair

The imported candidate provided a useful stop-and-check handshake but declared competing local `beforeSend` and `confirm` callbacks. Those callbacks omitted the exact R04 operation, attempt, dispatch owner, fence, expiry, and canonical transition time. It also required progress when only one Spectrum part remained and fabricated per-part callbacks around Advanced iMessage's provider-atomic multipart call. All three behaviors conflicted with the frozen repair contract.

`PhotonDeliveryRuntimeProgress` now carries the canonical `PhotonDeliveryProgressPort`, the exact `DeliveryDispatchClaim` obtained from R04, and an injected `now()` function. For every sequential Spectrum part when more than one logical part remains, the client passes the unchanged full operation, including its attempt ID, the unchanged claim, that logical part index, and a fresh canonical time to `assertCanContinue`. After validating provider acceptance, it passes the same authority fields, returned `MessagePartReference`, and another fresh time to `recordConfirmedPart`. A rejection becomes ambiguous evidence and prevents every later provider write.

`withPhotonDeliveryRuntimeProgress` attaches this envelope under a module-private symbol on a copied dispatch object. It is non-enumerable and therefore cannot enter the serializable `PhotonDeliveryDispatch`, operation, provider request, outcome, receipt, or durable state. The shared wire-shaped dispatch type remains unchanged.

Spectrum multipart requires the envelope only when more than one unresolved logical part will be sent sequentially. One remaining part does not invoke either callback even if an envelope is present. Advanced multipart remains one provider-atomic `sendMultipart` call and never invokes sequential part callbacks.
